Why it exists

ICMLE

Try it first

Spend two or three minutes on this before reading anything else.

The interviewer says: "Design a system that stops counterfeit listings on a marketplace." Ten million listings are created a day. You have 45 minutes.

Before you draw a single box, write down the five questions whose answers change the design. For each, say what would change.

Show answerHide answer
  1. What decision does the system make, and when? Block at publish, hide pending review, or flag after publication. Blocking at publish means a latency budget of a few hundred milliseconds. Flagging afterwards allows a batch job and heavier models.
  2. What does each mistake cost? A missed counterfeit (refunds, lost buyer trust, brand complaints) against a wrongly blocked genuine seller (lost sales, a support ticket, a seller who leaves). The ratio sets every threshold.
  3. How many human reviews are available a day? Review capacity, not model accuracy, usually decides how many listings get a careful look.
  4. Where do labels come from? Reviewer decisions, buyer reports, brand-owner takedowns, returns marked "not as described". Each arrives at a different delay and covers a different slice, and reviewers only label what the model sent them.
  5. How will we know it works? An offline metric at the operating point, an online experiment, and a guardrail on genuine sellers.

Candidates who start with "I would use a CNN on the images" have answered question six before asking the first five. The interviewer is grading the order.

Two analogies

Airport security. Every passenger walks through a cheap metal detector. A few are sent to a slower, more thorough search. Very few are refused boarding. The lanes are sized to the number of officers, and the threshold for a second look is set so the queue keeps moving.

A hospital emergency department. A triage nurse spends a minute on each arrival and assigns a band. Doctors' time goes where it changes outcomes most. That is not always the sickest patients: someone certain to need surgery goes straight to the surgical team without a long assessment.

What does the emergency department teach that the airport does not? One sentence.

Compare with mineHide

Scarce expert time is best spent where the decision is uncertain and the stakes are high, not on the cases the cheap check is already sure about.

Where each analogy breaks

  • Passengers do not adapt. Counterfeiters do. Block one pattern and the next week's listings avoid it, so a fraud model decays faster than most.
  • Triage is labelled by outcomes. A listing that was allowed and never reported is not known to be genuine: the labels come mostly from what was reviewed.
  • Both have fixed queues. Listing volume spikes in sale seasons, and the threshold that fit the capacity in March overflows it in October.

Self-test

Why?Why should reviewer time not go first to the listings the model is surest about?

Show answerHide answer

Those can be blocked automatically at little cost, because the model is almost always right about them. A review there changes few decisions. Reviews change most decisions in the uncertain middle.

Concept

1 clarify decision, timing, cost of each error, capacity, scale, latency 2 metrics offline: precision and recall at the operating point online: counterfeits reaching buyers business: loss a day · guardrail: genuine blocked 3 data label sources, their delays, and what never gets a label 4 features text, images, price against the category, seller history, network signals 5 model baseline first (rules, logistic regression), then boosting, then image and text in a cascade 6 serving at publish within the latency budget, or after; cheap model on all, expensive on the uncertain 7 iterate shadow mode, experiment, monitoring, retraining for each listing: allow cost p × C_miss block cost (1 − p) × C_block review cost C_review, while capacity lasts
  • Operating point. The threshold where the system actually acts. A model's AUC is a summary over all thresholds; the business lives at one or two of them, set by costs and capacity, as in precision, recall and the threshold.
  • Cascade. A cheap stage scores everything and passes only the uncertain or risky share to an expensive stage. Total cost and latency are dominated by the first stage.
  • Capacity-constrained triage. With unlimited reviewers, review whenever it is cheaper than the best automatic action. With limited reviewers, spend them where they save the most money per review.
  • Shadow mode. Run the new model alongside the old one and log what it would have done, before it acts. It catches broken features and threshold surprises without risk.
  • Feedback loop. The model decides which listings get reviewed, and reviews become labels. Without a small random sample of unflagged listings, the training data stops covering what the model lets through: see feedback loops and selective labels.

Self-test

A missed counterfeit costs ₹2,000, a wrong block ₹300 and a review ₹40. Above what probability should a listing be blocked rather than allowed, if there were no reviewers?

Show answerHide answer

Block when (1 − p) × 300 < p × 2,000, so p > 300 / 2,300 = 13%. The threshold is far below 50% because a miss costs nearly seven times a wrong block.

Mechanics, faded

Worked: triage with 80,000 reviews a day

The model sorts the day's listings into five bands. Reviewers are assumed to decide correctly. Costs: miss ₹2,000, wrong block ₹300, review ₹40.

Medium0.2060,00012,000
Low0.052,00,00010,000
Very low0.00296,90,00019,380

Without capacity limits. Review beats allowing when p > 40 / 2,000 = 2%, and blocking beats reviewing when p > 1 − 40 / 300 = 87%. So: block the very high band, review high, medium and low, allow very low. That needs 2,90,000 reviews.

With 80,000. Rank the bands by what a review saves over the best automatic action:

  • Medium: the best automatic action is to block, at (1 − 0.2) × 300 = ₹240. A review saves ₹200.
  • High: block, ₹120. A review saves ₹80.
  • Low: allow, 0.05 × 2,000 = ₹100. A review saves ₹60.
  • Very high: block, ₹15. A review would cost more than it saves.

So review all 60,000 medium listings and 20,000 of the high band, block the rest of the high band and all of the very high band, and allow the low and very low bands. Cost: ₹634.6 lakh a day, against ₹1,567.6 lakh for allowing everything. Genuine listings blocked: 5,000.

The intuitive plan, reviewing from the top band down, spends 50,000 reviews on listings the model was already sure about. It costs ₹691.6 lakh and blocks 24,000 genuine listings. Reviewers belong in the uncertain middle.

Half-worked: where the remaining loss lives

Of the ₹634.6 lakh, how much comes from the low and very low bands, which are allowed? What does that say about where to invest next?

Show answerHide answer

Low: 2,00,000 × 0.05 × 2,000 = ₹200 lakh. Very low: 96,90,000 × 0.002 × 2,000 = ₹387.6 lakh. Together ₹587.6 lakh, 93% of the loss. More reviewers barely help: 1,50,000 reviews a day cut the cost only to ₹590.6 lakh. The money is in the 29,380 counterfeits the model scores as safe. The next investment is a better model for the bottom bands: image similarity to known brand products, price far below the category, new sellers listing many luxury items.

Bare: sizing the image model

Each listing has five images. An image model on one GPU handles 400 images a second. Traffic peaks at three times the daily average. How many GPUs to score every image at publish? And if a light model first passes only 20% of listings to the image model?

Show answerHide answer

50,000,000 images a day is 579 a second on average and 1,736 at peak: 4.3 GPUs, so 5, plus one spare for failures, 6. With the cascade, peak load is 347 images a second: 1 GPU plus a spare, 2. The cascade cuts serving cost by two thirds, and the light model must be checked for counterfeits it waves through, because the image model never sees them.

Predict, then play

Cut review capacity from 80,000 to 50,000 a day. Predict what happens to the daily cost and to the number of genuine listings blocked.

Show answerHide answer

Cost rises only from ₹634.6 to ₹670.6 lakh, but genuine listings blocked rise from 5,000 to 21,000: the medium listings no longer reviewed are blocked, because blocking a 20% band is cheaper than letting it through. The money metric barely moves while the guardrail quadruples, which is why the design needs both.

bands = [(.95, 20_000), (.60, 30_000), (.20, 60_000), (.05, 200_000), (.002, 9_690_000)]
MISS, BLOCK, REVIEW, CAP = 2000, 300, 40, 80_000

auto = lambda p: min(p * MISS, (1 - p) * BLOCK)            # best action without a reviewer
order = sorted(bands, key=lambda b: REVIEW - auto(b[0]))   # biggest saving per review first
cost, left = 0, CAP
for p, n in order:
    r = min(n, left) if auto(p) > REVIEW else 0
    left -= r
    cost += r * REVIEW + (n - r) * auto(p)
cost / 1e5                                                 # 634.6 lakh a day

Self-test

Why?Why does the very high band get no reviews even when capacity is plentiful?

Show answerHide answer

Blocking it costs (1 − 0.95) × 300 = ₹15 a listing, less than the ₹40 review. The model is right often enough that a review cannot pay for itself.

The worked plan assumes reviewers are always right. Reviewers miss 10% of counterfeits. What changes?

Show answerHide answer

A review now costs ₹40 plus 10% of the counterfeits slipping through, so it saves less in every band. Bands near the edges lose their reviews first, and measuring reviewer accuracy with audits becomes part of the design.

Self-test

Recall on reviewed listings is steady at 85%, but buyer reports of counterfeits are rising. Explain.

Show answerHide answer

Recall is measured only on what the model sent for review. Counterfeits it scores low are never reviewed, so their rise is invisible to the metric. A random audit of allowed listings would show it.

Applications in pairs

Pair 1

Counterfeit listings. Review queue limited to 80,000 a day.

Payment fraud. Manual checks limited by an operations team.

Pair 2

Search ranking. Retrieve a thousand candidates, rank the top hundred carefully.

Content moderation for an LLM feature. A cheap classifier, then a larger model on borderline outputs.

What does each pair share?Hide

Pair 1 is capacity-constrained triage. Costs set the thresholds; capacity decides where human attention goes; reviewed cases become biased labels.

Pair 2 is a cascade. A cheap stage on everything and an expensive one on a small share. In search the cheap stage is retrieval; in moderation it is a small classifier. In both, the end-to-end recall is capped by the first stage.

A well-known misuse

Presenting the model as the system. A design that ends at "train a classifier with 0.97 AUC" has said nothing about thresholds, capacity, latency, labels or monitoring, which is where these systems succeed or fail. A widely cited paper from Google argues that the model code is a small fraction of a real ML system, and that most of the risk lives in the data dependencies and glue around it. 1

Self-test

An interviewer asks for your model's AUC. What do you say after the number?

Show answerHide answer

The precision and recall at the operating point, how that point was chosen from costs and capacity, and how the number was measured given that labels come from reviews.

In practice

  • Spend the first five minutes on the decision, the costs and the capacity. Write them on the board.
  • Start with a baseline you could ship in a week, then say what would justify each step up. Google's guidance puts a simple heuristic or model in production before machine learning gets clever. 2
  • Set thresholds from costs and capacity, and recompute them when either changes.
  • Audit a random sample of what the system allows. It is the only unbiased recall.
  • Launch in shadow mode, then experiment with buyer-side outcomes as the primary metric and blocked genuine sellers as the guardrail.
  • Plan the decay. Retraining cadence, drift alerts and a playbook for a new fraud pattern: see monitoring and drift.

Self-test

Name the one number you would put on the team's weekly dashboard to show the system is working, and why not precision.

Show answerHide answer

Counterfeits reaching buyers per million orders, estimated from audits and buyer reports. Precision can rise while more counterfeits get through, if the model simply flags less.

Nuances and contested ground

  • Block automatically or always review? One view: an automated block is a judgment about a seller's livelihood and should have a human in the loop. The other: at millions a day, insisting on review means most listings get no check at all. Appeals and audit trails are the usual compromise.
  • Explainable or accurate? Rules and simple models are easy to explain to sellers and regulators. Deep models catch more. Many teams use the deep model to rank and show reviewers the evidence, not the score.
  • Build or buy? Vendors bring cross-company fraud signals; in-house teams know the marketplace's own patterns. The answer often changes with scale.
  • How much to tell sellers? Specific reasons help honest sellers fix listings and teach counterfeiters how to evade.

Self-test

Give the strongest case for always having a human confirm a block.

Show answerHide answer

A wrong block can cost a small seller their income and is hard to reverse in their customers' eyes. Human confirmation catches model failures that look confident, and supports a fair appeal. The cost is capacity, so it fits when blocks are rare.

Interview ladder

Answer each one out loud before opening it. The follow-ups are where interviews are decided.

Rung 1 · Recall the idea

Walk me through how you structure any ML system design answer.

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Where do most candidates lose time?
  • What do you write down before choosing a model?

Weak

Starts with the model architecture.

Strong

Clarify the decision, timing, error costs, capacity, scale and latency. Then metrics at the operating point and a guardrail; data and label sources with their biases; features; a baseline model and what justifies the next one; serving and a cascade; then shadow mode, experiment, monitoring and retraining. Says which of these the interviewer seems to care about and spends the time there.

Rung 2 · Why

Why would you review medium-risk listings before high-risk ones when reviewers are scarce?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • When would you review the high band first?
  • What happens to the medium band when there are no reviewers?

Model answer

a review is worth what it saves over the best automatic action. High-risk listings can be blocked cheaply because the model is usually right; medium-risk ones are expensive whichever way you act automatically. With a miss at ₹2,000, a wrong block at ₹300 and a review at ₹40, a medium review saves ₹200 and a high one ₹80.

Weak

"Highest risk first" without reasoning about the automatic alternative.

Strong

Must have. Compares each band's review cost with its best automatic action.

Strong signal. Notes the effect on genuine sellers blocked, and that the answer flips if blocks become much more expensive.

Rung 3 · What if

Your counterfeit model's recall on reviewed listings is steady, but complaints from buyers are rising. What is happening?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • How would you measure true recall?
  • How big must the audit sample be?

Weak

"Retrain the model."

Strong

Selective labels: recall is measured only where the model sent listings, so new counterfeits it scores low never appear. Start a daily random audit of allowed listings to estimate true recall and find the new pattern, then add those labels to training. Size the audit from the rare rate: finding a 0.2% pattern needs thousands of reviews.

Rung 4 · DesignMLE

Design the serving path for scoring 10 million listings a day at publish within 200 milliseconds.

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Images are the best signal but the model takes 80 ms. Where does it go?
  • What happens when the image service is down?

Weak

One large model behind an API.

Strong

A cascade: rules and a boosted model on precomputed seller features synchronously, well inside the budget; the image model only on the uncertain share, either synchronously within the remaining budget or asynchronously with the listing held as pending. Sizing from peak load, not average. A fallback that fails safe by holding risky listings rather than allowing everything. Feature freshness from a feature store with point-in-time training data.

Rung 5 · JudgmentICMLELead

Operations wants to cut review capacity from 80,000 to 50,000 a day because the model is now very accurate. What do you tell them?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • They point to the cost metric, which barely moves.
  • What would make you agree?

Weak

Agrees because the cost metric is flat, or refuses without numbers.

Strong

Runs the triage at the lower capacity: in the worked case cost rises 6% but genuine listings blocked quadruple, from 5,000 to 21,000 a day, which shows up later as seller churn and support load. Presents both numbers, proposes the cut with a guardrail on wrongful blocks and an appeal-rate threshold, or invests the savings in the bottom bands where 93% of the loss lives.

Your one-liner

Without scrolling up, write the whole topic in one sentence.

Compare with mineHide

An ML system is designed from the decision outward: the costs of each error and the review capacity set the thresholds, a cascade keeps serving cheap, scarce reviewers go to the uncertain middle, and a random audit is the only honest measure of what the system lets through.
