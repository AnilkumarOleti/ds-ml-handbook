Why it exists

1

Try it first

Spend two or three minutes on this before reading anything else.

You are asked to set up product analytics for six product squads: search, listings, checkout, payments, seller tools and buyer growth. You have eight headcount, four of them still to hire. Today every squad sends requests to a shared inbox, waits a week, and each squad defines "active seller" its own way.

Choose an operating model: central (one team, one queue), embedded (an analyst in each squad, reporting to the squad), or hub and spoke (analysts sit in squads and report to you, with a small central hub). Say who does what with the eight, and what you will have fixed in 90 days.

Show answerHide answer

Hub and spoke. Six analysts, one per squad, sit in squad rituals and report to you. Two form the hub: one owns the metric layer, definitions and experiment review; one takes cross-squad questions and covers absences.

  • Why not central: a shared queue is why squads wait a week. Analysts never learn a product well enough to suggest the question.
  • Why not fully embedded: an analyst whose review is written by the squad lead whose launches they judge is under pressure, and six squads drift into six definitions of every metric.
  • The hub keeps one definition of each metric and reviews readouts from outside the squad.

In 90 days: one definition each of the ten metrics leadership reads; an experiment brief template with a decision rule; the four hires made; each squad's analyst able to name the two decisions they changed. Not in 90 days: a new data platform.

Two analogies

The hospital. Ward doctors are embedded with patients and know each case. The pathology lab is central: it runs tests for every ward to one standard. A hospital with only ward doctors gets inconsistent tests. One with only a lab has nobody who knows the patient.

The newspaper. Bureau correspondents live in their beats and find the stories. The central desk sets the style, checks facts and decides what runs. The correspondent reports to the desk, not to the people being covered.

What does the newspaper teach that the hospital does not? One sentence.

Compare with mineHide

The correspondent's independence comes from the reporting line: they live with the people they cover but answer to an editor, which is what lets an embedded analyst deliver an unwelcome result.

Where each analogy breaks

  • Doctors and journalists have professions that set standards. Analytics standards are mostly set by the team itself.
  • Bureaus are far apart. Squads share users and metrics, so their work collides: a checkout win can be a payments loss.
  • Both are mature. A new analytics function also has to build the data it reports on.

Self-test

A squad lead asks for her analyst to report to her "so priorities are clear". What do you offer instead?

Show answerHide answer

Priorities set jointly each quarter, with the analyst in all her squad's rituals, while the reporting line stays with analytics so readouts stay independent and methods consistent.

Concept

operating model central · embedded · hub and spoke (seats in squads, reporting line central) the hub owns metric definitions · experiment review shared tools · the hiring bar squad analysts the squad's questions · decision briefs readouts · the squad's metric health hiring funnel entering a stage = hires ÷ product of the pass rates from that stage on hours = Σ entering × hours per candidate career paths IC ladder to staff and principal, manager ladder, equal pay at equal level first 90 days listen (30) · fix one pain, agree definitions (60) · set the cadence (90)
  • Central. Consistent methods, flexible allocation, shallow product knowledge and a queue. Suits a small team or a company with few products.
  • Embedded. Deep context and speed, divergent definitions, isolated analysts with no peer review, and a reporting line to the person being evaluated.
  • Hub and spoke. The common settlement: seats in squads, reporting line and standards central. Its cost is two bosses in practice, which needs a written agreement on priorities.
  • Analytics maturity. Companies that compete on analytics treat it as an enterprise capability with senior sponsorship, not as scattered local efforts. 1
  • The hiring bar is set by the questions you ask and the rubric you score against, written down before the first interview.

Self-test

What does a hub-and-spoke team gain over a fully embedded one, and what does it pay?

Show answerHide answer

It gains one set of definitions, peer review and independence of readouts. It pays in a double line of accountability that must be managed: the squad sets day-to-day priorities and the hub sets standards and the career.

Why?Why is the reporting line, rather than where people sit, the thing that protects independence?

Show answerHide answer

Whoever writes the appraisal shapes what an analyst is willing to say. Sitting with a squad builds context; reporting to it creates pressure to agree.

Mechanics, faded

Worked: the hiring funnel for four analysts

Past hiring gives these pass rates and interviewer hours per candidate entering each stage:

StagePass rateHours per candidateCandidates enteringInterviewer hours
Application screen30%0.124224
Phone screen50%1.07373
Take-home and review45%1.53654
Onsite loop35%5.01682
Offer accepted70%5.7 offers

Work backwards: 4 hires ÷ 0.70 = 5.7 offers; ÷ 0.35 = 16 onsites; ÷ 0.45 = 36 take-homes; ÷ 0.50 = 73 phone screens; ÷ 0.30 = 242 applications, about 60 per hire. Interviewer time: 233 hours, about six weeks of one person's time, spread over the team. The onsite loop is the largest single cost, and the place where a sharper earlier screen saves the most.

Half-worked: where the funnel leaks

Offer acceptance drops from 70% to 50% because a competitor pays more. Redo the applications and the interviewer hours. Then consider tightening the phone screen to pass 40%, which raises the take-home pass rate to 60%.

Show answerHide answer

At 50% acceptance: 8 offers, 339 applications and 326 hours, 40% more of everyone's time. Losing candidates at the last stage is the most expensive leak, because every earlier stage was spent on them. Fix pay or speed before anything else. The tighter phone screen, at 70% acceptance, cuts hours from 233 to 213: fewer take-homes are reviewed for the same hires.

Bare: the first quarter's output

The four new analysts start together. Assume they produce half an experienced analyst's output in months one and two, and 80% in month three. How much do the four add in the first quarter, in analyst-months, and what do you promise the squads?

Show answerHide answer

4 × (0.5 + 0.5 + 0.8) = 7.2 analyst-months of 12, and they also take time from the experienced four who onboard them. Promise the squads named analysts in month one and full service from month four. Stagger starts by a few weeks if you can, so onboarding does not land on the same mentors at once.

Predict, then play

Raise the onsite pass rate from 35% to 50% by making the take-home more selective. Predict how much applications and interviewer hours fall.

Show answerHide answer

Both fall by the same 30%: every stage before the offer scales with the number of onsites needed. Applications fall from 242 to 169 and hours from 233 to 163. An efficient funnel depends on early stages that predict the late ones.

Self-test

Why?Why is a candidate who declines your offer the most expensive outcome in the funnel?

Show answerHide answer

Every stage before the offer was spent on them, including the onsite, the costliest. One decline at the end costs as much as several rejections at the start.

Self-test

A year in, the team is busy and well liked, but the business head cannot name anything it changed. What do you fix first?

Show answerHide answer

Start a decision log: for each piece of work, the decision it informed and what happened. Then redirect effort towards work tied to decisions. The team has become a service desk.

Self-test

The company grows from six squads to twenty. What happens to your hub?

Show answerHide answer

It splits into a metrics and platform group and a methods group for experiment review, and squads are grouped into areas, each with a lead analyst. The hub must grow slower than the spokes or it becomes a bureaucracy.

Applications in pairs

Pair 1

Setting up analytics at a new capability centre. Stakeholders are in another time zone.

Inheriting a team of ten. Half of them maintain dashboards nobody reads.

Pair 2

Hiring your first staff-level IC.

Promoting a strong analyst to manager.

What does each pair share?Hide

Pair 1 is earning a seat at the decision. Distance and legacy work both push a team towards output nobody uses. In both, find the two or three decisions that matter most to each stakeholder, attach a named person to each, and retire work that serves none. A remote team needs written decision briefs and overlap hours more than a co-located one.

Pair 2 is the career ladder. A staff IC needs a scope larger than one squad, such as owning experimentation methods, or the role is a title only. Promotion to manager should be a choice between two ladders of equal standing, not the only way up.

A well-known misuse

Measuring the team by requests closed. It rewards fast, small, uncontroversial work and penalises the analyst who spends three weeks on the one question that changes the roadmap. Measure decisions informed and their value, imperfect as that is.

Self-test

Your stakeholders are mostly in the US and your team is in India. Name two practices that matter more than in a co-located team.

Show answerHide answer

Written decision briefs agreed before the work starts, and readouts sent as documents before a short overlap-hour meeting. Asynchronous writing replaces the hallway conversation.

Lead

In practice

  • Spend the first 30 days listening: each stakeholder's top three decisions and what they currently use to make them.
  • Fix one visible pain by day 60, such as one definition of active seller, to earn trust for the larger changes.
  • Write the rubric before the first interview: problem framing, statistics, SQL, communication, each with weak and strong answers. The ladders in this handbook are a template.
  • Keep a decision log, and review it with leadership each quarter.
  • Protect deep work. Reserve a share of each analyst's time for proactive analysis the squad did not ask for.
  • Build two ladders, IC and manager, with equal levels and pay.

Self-test

What does a quarterly review of the decision log give you that a satisfaction survey does not?

Show answerHide answer

Evidence of impact, in decisions and money, that survives a budget review. A survey shows whether people like the team, which is a different thing.

Nuances and contested ground

  • Embedded or central? Advocates of embedding say context is everything and central teams become ticket queues. Advocates of centralisation say embedded analysts lose independence and standards. The hub-and-spoke model is a compromise, and its success depends on the written agreement between squad leads and the head of analytics.
  • Specialists or generalists? Some teams hire separate experimentation, modelling and analytics engineering roles. Others hire full-stack analysts who do all three. Specialists go deeper; generalists finish work without hand-offs.
  • Take-home exercises? They test real work and favour candidates with free time. Live exercises are fairer on time and more stressful. Paid or short take-homes are a middle path.
  • Who owns the metric layer? Analytics, data engineering or a platform team. Ownership by analytics keeps definitions close to their use; by engineering, close to their production.

Self-test

Make the case for a fully embedded model.

Show answerHide answer

Analysts who report to the squad are part of its decisions from the start, are never stuck in a queue, and are judged on the squad's outcomes. Standards can be kept through a guild and shared tools without a central reporting line.

Interview ladder

Answer each one out loud before opening it. The follow-ups are where interviews are decided.

Rung 1 · Recall the idea

Compare central, embedded and hub-and-spoke analytics teams.

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Which would you choose for six squads and eight people?
  • What does each model's failure look like?

Weak

Lists the three with no criteria.

Strong

Central: consistency and flexible allocation, at the cost of a queue and shallow context. Embedded: context and speed, at the cost of drifting definitions and independence. Hub and spoke: seats in squads, reporting line and standards central, at the cost of managing two lines of accountability. Chooses by team size, product count and how mature the shared standards are.

Rung 2 · Why

Why should embedded analysts report to the head of analytics rather than to the squad lead?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • The squad lead says she cannot set priorities otherwise.
  • When would you let them report to the squad?

Model answer

whoever writes the appraisal shapes what an analyst is willing to say, and analysts often judge the launches of the person they would report to. A central line protects unwelcome readouts, keeps methods and definitions consistent, and gives a career path. Priorities are set jointly with the squad each quarter.

"For consistency."

Strong

Must have. Independence of readouts tied to the appraisal.

Strong signal. Offers a joint priority-setting process so the squad lead keeps control of what gets worked on.

Rung 3 · What if

Offer acceptance fell from 70% to 50%. What does that do to your hiring plan for four analysts?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Where else in the funnel would you look?
  • What would you change first?

Weak

"Interview more people."

Strong

Works back through the funnel: offers rise from 5.7 to 8, applications from 242 to 339, interviewer hours from 233 to 326. Treats late-stage loss as the costliest leak, so first checks pay, speed and the candidate experience; then tightens early stages so the onsite pass rate rises.

Rung 4 · Design

Design the first 90 days of a new product analytics function.

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • What will you not do in 90 days?
  • How will leadership know it is working?

Weak

"Build dashboards for every squad."

Strong

Days 1–30: interview each stakeholder for their top decisions, audit existing metrics and requests. Days 31–60: one agreed definition of the leadership metrics, a decision-brief template, the operating model agreed in writing, hiring started with a rubric. Days 61–90: each squad's analyst attached to named decisions, a weekly readout cadence, a decision log. Not in 90 days: a new data platform. Success is decisions changed, reported quarterly.

Rung 5 · Judgment

Budgets are cut and you must lose two of eight analysts. How do you decide what the team stops doing?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • A squad lead says losing their analyst will hurt the roadmap.
  • How do you tell the team?

Weak

Cuts evenly across squads.

Strong

Uses the decision log: what each piece of work changed and what it was worth. Stops work that informs no decision, moves recurring reporting to self-serve, and covers the lowest-value squads from the hub rather than leaving none. States openly to leadership what will no longer be possible. Tells the team the reasons and criteria in person before any announcement.
