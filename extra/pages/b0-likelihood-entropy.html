Likelihood, cross-entropy and KL divergence

Try it first

Spend two or three minutes on this before reading anything else.

Two fraud models score four transactions. Only the first is fraud.

TransactionTruthModel AModel B
1Fraud0.600.95
2Genuine0.400.05
3Genuine0.400.05
4Genuine0.400.70

At a 0.5 threshold, what is each model's accuracy? Now score each by log loss, the average of −ln(probability given to what actually happened). Which model would you rather have pricing risk on your marketplace?

Show answerHide answer

Accuracy: A gets 4 of 4. B gets 3 of 4, because it puts 0.70 on a genuine transaction.

Log loss: A gives 0.60 to the truth every time: −ln 0.60 = 0.511. B gives 0.95 to the truth three times and 0.30 once: (3 × 0.051 + 1.204) / 4 = 0.339.

The two scores disagree. Accuracy only asks which side of 0.5 each score falls. Log loss asks how much probability the model put on what happened. B is nearly certain and right three times, and its one mistake is not a confident one. A is never wrong and never sure.

For pricing risk you want B: its numbers can be multiplied by a loss to get an expected cost, and A's cannot. Notice also where B's loss comes from: 1.204 of its 1.358 total, almost 90%, is the one transaction. Log loss is dominated by the few cases a model gets wrong with confidence.

Two analogies

The forecaster scored by surprise. A weather forecaster says 90% rain. If it rains she is barely surprised. If it stays dry she is very surprised. Score her by how surprised she was on each day, averaged over a season. Saying 50% every day keeps her safe from big surprises and earns a mediocre score. Saying 99% when she should have said 70% earns the occasional enormous one.

The phrasebook for the wrong country. A traveller packs a phrasebook that gives the shortest phrases to the things it expects you to say most. Packed for Spain and used in Spain, conversations are as short as they can be. Used in Portugal, the phrases still work, but every exchange runs longer. The extra length is the price of preparing for the wrong place.

What does the phrasebook show that the forecaster does not? One sentence.

Compare with mineHide

Even a perfect forecaster has a floor of unavoidable surprise, the entropy, and the phrasebook separates that floor from the extra cost of the wrong beliefs, which is the KL divergence and the only part a better model can remove.

Where each analogy breaks

  • The forecaster is scored on one kind of event. A model's log loss averages over many examples with different true probabilities, so a single number hides which examples carry the loss.
  • Phrase lengths are whole words. Information is measured in fractions of a bit or a nat, and the formulas allow any length.
  • Both treat the true odds as knowable. In practice you only ever see outcomes, never p, so entropy is estimated, and KL is only defined between distributions you have written down.

Self-test

The forecaster says 100% rain and it stays dry. What is her log loss for the day, and what does a training library do about it?

Show answerHide answer

−ln 0, which is infinite. Libraries clip probabilities to a range such as 10⁻¹⁵ to 1 − 10⁻¹⁵, so one confident miss costs about 34.5 nats instead of breaking the average.

Why?Why does a forecaster who always says the base rate never get a terrible day?

Show answerHide answer

She never puts a tiny probability on anything that happens often, so her surprise is bounded. Her average surprise equals the entropy of the outcome, which is the score to beat.

Concept

likelihood L(θ) = Π P(xᵢ | θ) a function of θ, data fixed log-likelihood ℓ(θ) = Σ ln P(xᵢ | θ) sums beat products MLE θ̂ = argmax ℓ(θ) Bernoulli: θ̂ = k / n MAP θ̂ = argmax [ℓ(θ) + ln P(θ)] the prior is a penalty entropy H(p) = − Σ p(x) ln p(x) cross-entropy H(p, q) = − Σ p(x) ln q(x) = log loss, averaged KL divergence KL(p ‖ q) = Σ p(x) ln( p(x) / q(x) ) = H(p, q) − H(p) ≥ 0, not symmetric
  • Likelihood. How probable the observed data is under a parameter value. It is not the probability of the parameter, and it does not sum to one over θ.
  • Maximum likelihood. Pick the parameter that makes the data most probable. For a conversion rate it is the observed share. For a linear model with normal noise of fixed spread it is least squares. For a classifier it is minimum log loss.
  • MAP. Add the log of a prior and maximise. A normal prior on weights adds −λΣw², which is L2 regularisation. A Laplace prior adds −λΣ|w|, which is L1.
  • Entropy. The average surprise when you know the true distribution. A fair coin has 1 bit, or 0.693 nats. A label that is 2% positive has 0.141 bits. 1
  • Cross-entropy. The average surprise when outcomes come from p and you predict q. Minimising it over q is the same as maximising the likelihood, because the training labels are draws from p.
  • KL divergence. The gap between the two. It is zero only when q = p. The direction matters: KL(p ‖ q) punishes q for putting little mass where p has a lot. 3

The chain to carry into an interview: minimise log loss = maximise likelihood = minimise KL from the data to the model. The entropy term is fixed by the data, so the three move together.

Self-test

Why?Why can likelihood not be read as the probability that θ is the true value?

Show answerHide answer

It is P(data | θ). Turning it into P(θ | data) needs a prior through Bayes' rule, exactly as a p-value cannot be read as the probability of the null.

A model's log loss is 0.110 on data whose labels are 2% positive. Split it into the part a better model could remove and the part it could not.

Show answerHide answer

The entropy of a 2% label is 0.098 nats, which no model can beat without better features. The remaining 0.012 is the KL divergence, the room left for improvement.

Mechanics, faded

Worked: the likelihood of a conversion rate

A new seller tool is shown to 200 sellers and 12 use it. The log-likelihood of a rate p is 12 ln p + 188 ln(1 − p). It peaks at p̂ = 12 / 200 = 6%.

The data favour 6% over 5% by a likelihood ratio of only 1.22, and over 3% by 11.2. A common summary is the set of p whose log-likelihood is within 1.92 of the peak, which matches a 95% interval in large samples: 3.3% to 9.9%. It is lopsided, wider above 6% than below, because a rate cannot go below zero. The symmetric normal interval, 2.7% to 9.3%, ignores that.

Half-worked: a prior pulls the estimate

Past seller tools were used by about 5% of sellers. Encode that as a Beta(2, 38) prior. The MAP estimate is (k + a − 1) / (n + a + b − 2) = (12 + 1) / (200 + 38). Finish it, then redo it for 1 user out of 10.

Show answerHide answer

13 / 238 = 5.5%, close to the 6% the data say, because 200 sellers outweigh a prior worth about 40. With 1 of 10, the MLE is 10% and the MAP is 2 / 48 = 4.2%. With little data the prior dominates. This is the mechanism behind shrinkage in partial pooling.

Bare: the log loss to beat

Fraud is 2% of transactions. A model reports a validation log loss of 0.09. Is that good? Find the log loss of a model that predicts 0.02 for every transaction, and say how much better 0.09 is.

Show answerHide answer

Predicting the base rate gives the entropy: −(0.02 ln 0.02 + 0.98 ln 0.98) = 0.098. The model is only 8% better than knowing nothing about individual transactions. At a 50% base rate the same baseline is 0.693, so 0.09 would be superb. Always report log loss next to the base-rate log loss, or as the share of it removed.

Bare: KL in both directions

Training data had 2% fraud. Live traffic has 5%. Compute KL(live ‖ train) and KL(train ‖ live) for the label alone.

Show answerHide answer

KL(0.05 ‖ 0.02) = 0.05 ln 2.5 + 0.95 ln(0.95 / 0.98) = 0.0163. KL(0.02 ‖ 0.05) = 0.0121. The direction changes the number. Drift monitors such as the population stability index add the two directions together to avoid choosing.

Predict, then play

Keep the observed rate at 6% and raise the sample from 200 to 2,000. Predict how much narrower the likelihood interval gets.

Show answerHide answer

About √10 ≈ 3.2 times narrower, from 3.3%–9.9% to 5.0%–7.1%, and nearly symmetric. More data sharpens the peak of the log-likelihood, which is the same √n you met in the central limit theorem.

import numpy as np

y = np.array([1, 0, 0, 0])
def log_loss(p, eps=1e-15):
    p = np.clip(p, eps, 1 - eps)
    return -np.mean(y * np.log(p) + (1 - y) * np.log(1 - p))

log_loss(np.array([.60, .40, .40, .40]))   # 0.511
log_loss(np.array([.95, .05, .05, .70]))   # 0.339
log_loss(np.full(4, y.mean()))             # 0.562, the base-rate model for this tiny sample

Self-test

A model trained by minimising mean squared error on 0/1 labels. Is it doing maximum likelihood?

Show answerHide answer

Not for a Bernoulli outcome. Squared error is the likelihood for normal noise. On 0/1 labels it is the Brier score, a proper score but a different one, and it punishes confident mistakes far less than log loss.

Why?Why does the interval for 12 of 200 extend further above 6% than below?

Show answerHide answer

The log-likelihood is steeper towards zero, because a rate near zero makes 12 successes very unlikely, and flatter above. The interval follows the curve, so it is lopsided.

The row that bites in interviews is the misspecified one. Maximum likelihood always returns an answer. It says nothing about whether the model could have been right.

Self-test

A logistic regression on a small fraud sample returns a coefficient of 41 with a standard error of 9,000. What happened and what do you do?

Show answerHide answer

A feature separates the classes perfectly in this sample, so the likelihood keeps rising as the coefficient grows. Add an L2 penalty, which is a prior that makes the maximum finite, or merge rare categories.

Applications in pairs

Pair 1

Training a logistic regression. Minimise log loss on clicks.

Pretraining a language model. Minimise cross-entropy on the next token.

Pair 2

Distillation. Train a small model to match a large model's predicted distribution.

Drift monitoring. Compare this week's feature distribution with the training one.

What does each pair share?Hide

Pair 1 is maximum likelihood. Both models are trained by the same objective: make the observed label probable. A language model's loss is often reported as perplexity, eloss. A loss of 2.3 nats per token is a perplexity of 10, as if the model were choosing evenly among 10 tokens.

Pair 2 is KL divergence between two distributions. Distillation minimises KL from the teacher to the student, so the student learns the teacher's uncertainty as well as its answers. Drift monitors compute a divergence between two histograms, often the symmetric population stability index used in monitoring and drift.

A well-known misuse

Comparing log loss across datasets. "The new model's log loss is 0.07, down from 0.10" can mean nothing if the validation period's fraud rate fell from 3% to 2%: the base-rate loss alone falls from 0.135 to 0.098. Compare each model's loss with the base-rate loss of the same data, or compare models on the same rows.

Self-test

A vendor says its model has a log loss of 0.05. What two numbers do you ask for?

Show answerHide answer

The base rate of the evaluation data, to compute the log loss of predicting it, and the log loss of your current model on the same rows.

In practice

  • Write the likelihood before choosing the loss. Counts suggest Poisson, yes/no suggests Bernoulli, positive skewed amounts suggest a lognormal or gamma. The loss follows.
  • Report log loss with its baseline. "0.090 against 0.098 for the base rate" is honest. "0.09" alone is not.
  • Look at the worst 20 examples by loss. They are usually label errors, leakage or a missing feature.
  • Clip predictions and check calibration. A model that is right but overconfident loses on log loss and on decisions: see calibration.
  • Read regularisation as a prior. It makes the strength of λ a statement about how large you expect effects to be, which is easier to defend.
  • Use likelihood ratios to compare nested models. Twice the gain in log-likelihood is roughly chi-square with as many degrees of freedom as added parameters.

Self-test

Two models differ by one feature. The larger one improves the total log-likelihood on 10,000 rows by 1.5. Is the feature worth keeping on that evidence?

Show answerHide answer

Twice the gain is 3.0, against a chi-square critical value of 3.84 at 5% with one degree of freedom. Not convincing. Check it on held-out data, where the gain may vanish.

Nuances and contested ground

  • MLE or Bayesian? One camp prefers the MLE because it needs no prior and is what most libraries compute. The other notes that for small samples and many parameters a prior is not optional, and that regularised models are already MAP estimates with an unstated prior. In large-sample product work they usually agree.
  • Which direction of KL? Minimising KL(p ‖ q) makes q cover all of p's mass and can blur across modes. Minimising KL(q ‖ p) lets q settle on one mode. Variational inference uses the second and is criticised for understating uncertainty.
  • Log loss or Brier for model selection? Both are proper scores, which reward honest probabilities. 2 Log loss weighs rare confident errors heavily, which suits fraud and safety. Brier is bounded and more stable on small validation sets.
  • Is perplexity a good LLM metric? It measures how well a model predicts text, which tracks fluency. It does not measure whether answers are correct or useful, which is why LLM evaluation needs task-level tests.

Self-test

Give one case where you would choose Brier score over log loss.

Show answerHide answer

Comparing models on a small validation set where one or two mislabelled rows would dominate log loss. Brier caps the damage of each row at 1.

Interview ladder

Answer each one out loud before opening it. The follow-ups are where interviews are decided.

Rung 1 · Recall the idea

What is maximum likelihood estimation, and what does it give for a conversion rate?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Is the likelihood a probability distribution over the parameter?
  • What does it give for a linear model with normal errors?

Weak

"It finds the most likely parameter."

Strong

It picks the parameter value under which the observed data are most probable: k / n for a rate. The likelihood is a function of the parameter with the data fixed, and it is not a probability of the parameter. With normal errors of fixed spread it reduces to least squares, and for a classifier to minimising log loss.

Rung 2 · Why

Why do we train classifiers with log loss rather than accuracy?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • When would accuracy and log loss pick different models?
  • What is the log loss of a model that always predicts the base rate?

Model answer

log loss is the negative log-likelihood of a Bernoulli outcome, so minimising it is maximum likelihood, and it is smooth, so gradients exist. Accuracy is flat almost everywhere and ignores how confident the model was. Log loss rewards honest probabilities, which matter whenever a score is multiplied by a cost or a price.

Weak

"Accuracy is not differentiable." True, and only half the reason.

Strong

Must have. Links log loss to likelihood and to probabilities that can be acted on.

Strong signal. Gives the base-rate baseline, 0.098 at 2%, and an example where accuracy and log loss disagree.

Rung 3 · What ifMLE

How are cross-entropy, entropy and KL divergence related, and why does it matter for training?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Which of the three can a better model reduce?
  • Is KL symmetric?

Weak

Gives three formulas without connecting them.

Strong

Cross-entropy = entropy + KL. The entropy is fixed by the data, so minimising cross-entropy is minimising KL from the data distribution to the model. Only the KL part can be reduced. KL is not symmetric: the direction decides whether the model covers every mode or settles on one.

Rung 4 · DesignICMLE

Your logistic regression returns a coefficient of 41 with an enormous standard error. Diagnose and fix it, in terms of the likelihood.

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Why does adding L2 fix it?
  • What prior does L2 correspond to?

Weak

"Drop the feature."

Strong

Perfect or near-perfect separation: the likelihood keeps increasing as the coefficient grows, so the maximum is at infinity. L2 adds −λw² to the log-likelihood, which is a normal prior centred on zero and makes the maximum finite. Alternatives: merge rare categories, or use Firth's penalised likelihood.

Rung 5 · JudgmentICMLE

A team reports that a new fraud model cut validation log loss from 0.10 to 0.07 and wants to replace the old one. What do you check before you agree?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • The validation months differ. Does that matter?
  • Log loss improved but precision at the review threshold did not. Which wins?

Weak

Accepts a 30% improvement.

Strong

Were both models scored on the same rows, and what is the base-rate loss of those rows? A fall in prevalence alone lowers log loss. Looks at calibration and at the largest per-row losses. Then checks the decision metric at the operating threshold, because a better log loss can come from better probabilities in a region where no decision is made.
