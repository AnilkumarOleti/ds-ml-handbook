Churn and propensity models

A churn model predicts who will leave, and it is useful for forecasting, staffing and understanding why. But the buyers most likely to leave are not the ones an offer will keep, so targeting needs uplift measured in a test, and every programme needs a holdout.

Try it first

Spend two or three minutes on this before reading anything else.

A churn model scores 100,000 active buyers; 25% will not buy in the next 90 days. Its AUC is 0.82. The retention team sends a win-back offer, costing ₹150 per buyer targeted, to the top three risk deciles. A saved buyer is worth ₹2,500.

The offer was tested with a random holdout in every decile. Churn by decile, and how many points the offer cut it:

Risk decile12345678910
Churn without offer62%45%35%30%27%22%10%8%6%5%
Cut by the offer4688752110

Is the current targeting profitable? Who should get the offer?

Show answerHide answer

Each targeted buyer costs ₹150 and is saved with probability equal to the cut, worth ₹2,500, so the offer pays where the cut exceeds 150 ÷ 2,500 = 6 points.

  • Top three deciles: −₹5 lakh, ₹0, +₹5 lakh: break-even overall. The riskiest decile loses money: many of those buyers leave whatever you send.
  • Deciles 3, 4 and 5 are where the offer works best: +₹12.5 lakh together.

The model ranks who will leave, which is not who the offer will keep. Use the churn score to understand and prioritise, and target the offer by measured uplift, or by an uplift model trained on this test. 1

Two analogies

A hospital's triage list. Ranking patients by how sick they are is not the same as ranking them by how much a treatment will help. The sickest may be beyond help; the healthiest do not need it. Treatment goes where it changes the outcome.

A teacher's at-risk list. A model can predict which pupils will fail. Extra tutoring helps the pupils who are struggling but reachable, not the ones who have stopped attending or the ones who will pass anyway.

What it is notWho the drug helpsWho tutoring helpsWho an offer keeps
The lost causesBeyond helpStopped attendingLeaving whatever you do
The sure thingsRecovering anywayPassing anywayStaying anyway

What do the two share? One sentence.

Compare with mineHide

A good prediction of who will have a bad outcome is a different thing from a prediction of who an intervention will change, and resources should follow the second.

Where each analogy breaks

  • Triage is about one patient at a time. Retention budgets are about portfolios: the decision is how many to target and in what order.
  • Sickness is observed. Churn in non-contractual businesses is defined by a choice of window: "no purchase in 90 days" is a definition, not an event.
  • Treatments have known side effects. Retention offers can train buyers to wait for discounts, a cost no single test sees.

Self-test

Why?Why might the highest-risk decile respond least to an offer?

Show answerHide answer

Many of those buyers have already decided to leave, for reasons such as moving, a bad experience or a competitor, that a coupon does not change. Risk and responsiveness are different properties.

Concept

set-up for a non-contractual business observation date t features: behaviour before t (e.g. last 180 days) label: churned = no purchase in (t, t + 90 days] population: buyers active before t validate on a LATER observation date, not a random split models logistic regression (baseline, interpretable) gradient boosting (usually more accurate) evaluate AUC; lift and capture by decile; calibration; stability across months using the score risk ranking who is likely to leave; sizing; reporting value-weighted risk expected loss = P(churn) × value targeting an action needs uplift: P(stay | offer) − P(stay | none)
  • Churn has to be defined in businesses without subscriptions. The window should match the purchase cycle: 90 days may be churn for groceries and normal for furniture. 2
  • Leakage is the classic failure: features computed after the observation date, such as "days since last visit" measured at label time. A random split hides it; a time split exposes it. See validation and leakage.
  • Lift and capture speak to the business: the top decile has 2.5 times the base churn rate and holds a quarter of churners.
  • Propensity models for purchase, upgrade or response use the same machinery with a different label. The same warning applies: propensity to act is not responsiveness to a nudge.
  • Calibration matters when scores feed expected-value calculations; see calibration.

Self-test

Why?A churn model has an AUC of 0.91 on a random split and 0.82 on next month's data. What is the likeliest reason?

Show answerHide answer

Leakage or drift: features that use information from the label period, or patterns that changed. A random split lets the model see the same time period in training and testing. Trust the time-split number.

Why multiply churn probability by customer value before ranking for a retention programme?

Show answerHide answer

Losing a high-value buyer costs more. Ranking by expected loss puts attention where the money is, though targeting an offer still needs uplift.

How it works

Mechanics, faded

Worked: lift and capture

Churn rates by decile sum to 250 over ten deciles: a base rate of 25%.

  • Top decile: 62%, a lift of 62 ÷ 25 = 2.5; it holds 62 ÷ 250 = 25% of all churners.
  • Top three deciles: (62 + 45 + 35) ÷ 250 = 57% of churners in 30% of buyers.

Those are the numbers for planning and staffing: who to watch, how many saves are possible, and which reasons dominate in the riskiest groups.

Half-worked: the economics per decile

With 10,000 buyers per decile, profit = 10,000 × (cut × ₹2,500 − ₹150). Decile 1: 10,000 × (0.04 × 2,500 − 150) = −₹5 lakh. Finish deciles 2 to 10 and find the most profitable set.

Show answerHide answer

Decile 2: ₹0. Deciles 3 and 4: +₹5 lakh each. Decile 5: +₹2.5 lakh. Decile 6: −₹2.5 lakh. Deciles 7 to 10: −₹10 to −₹15 lakh each. Best set: deciles 3 to 5, +₹12.5 lakh, against ₹0 for the current top three. The break-even cut is 6 points; only middle-risk buyers clear it. Notice that ranking by risk would never find this set without the test.

Bare: a suspicious feature

The model's most important feature is "number of support tickets in the last 7 days", computed from a table refreshed weekly. The model is scored on the 1st of each month. What do you check?

Show answerHide answer

Whether, in training, the 7-day window ended at the observation date or at the refresh date, which may fall inside the label window. If it peeked into the label period, it is leakage. Also whether tickets are a cause or a symptom: buyers who have already decided to leave complain first. As a predictor that is fine; as a reason to cut ticket volume, it is not.

Predict, then play

Predict what happens to the best targeting set if the offer's cost falls from ₹150 to ₹75.

Show answerHide answer

Break-even falls to 3 points, so deciles 1 to 6 all pay: the set widens to the top six, and decile 1 joins because a 4-point cut now covers its cost. The ranking of who to target is set by uplift; the cut-off is set by cost and value.

Self-test

Why?Why build training and test sets from different observation dates rather than a random split of one date?

Show answerHide answer

The model will be used on future dates. Behaviour and data pipelines drift, and some leakage only shows across time. A later date mimics deployment.

The retention team wants to judge the model by how many targeted buyers stayed. What is wrong with that?

Show answerHide answer

Many would have stayed anyway, and the model chose them because of who they were. Judge the programme by the difference between targeted buyers and a random holdout of similar buyers.

Self-test

30% of buyers labelled 'churned' buy again within the following 90 days. What does that suggest?

Show answerHide answer

The window is too short for this business, or seasonal buyers are being labelled as churned. Lengthen the window, or define churn relative to each buyer's own purchase cycle.

Applications in pairs

Pair 1

Account managers for sellers at risk. Limited staff.

A quarterly churn forecast for finance.

Pair 2

A win-back coupon.

A free trial of a premium subscription.

What does each pair share?Hide

Pair 1 uses risk directly. Staff attention and forecasts need to know who is likely to leave and how many; value-weighted risk ranks the accounts.

Pair 2 targets an offer. Who the offer changes is what matters, so a randomised test and an uplift ranking beat a risk ranking. Propensity to subscribe is not responsiveness to a trial.

A well-known misuse

"Our churn model saved 4,000 customers." The count is of targeted customers who stayed. Without a holdout, the number includes everyone who would have stayed anyway, and the riskiest-first targeting may even be losing money. Every retention programme needs a standing random holdout.

Self-test

How would you report a retention programme's impact credibly?

Show answerHide answer

Keep a random holdout among eligible buyers; report the difference in retention between targeted and held-out buyers, times the number targeted, minus the cost. Break it down by risk decile to show where it pays.

In practice

  • Define churn from the buying cycle, and check how many "churned" buyers come back.
  • Build features strictly before the observation date; validate on later dates.
  • Report lift, capture and calibration, not just AUC.
  • Use risk for attention and forecasts; use uplift for offers.
  • Keep a random holdout in every retention programme, in every decile.
  • Price the offer: break-even uplift = cost ÷ value of a saved buyer.
  • Monitor monthly: AUC, calibration, and the uplift by decile.

Self-test

Why keep a holdout in every decile rather than one overall?

Show answerHide answer

Uplift varies by decile, which is where the targeting decision is made. An overall holdout gives the average effect and hides that the top decile loses money while the middle pays.

Nuances and contested ground

  • Is churn prediction worth it if offers need uplift? Risk scores still guide staffing, forecasting and understanding. Some teams skip them for targeting and go straight to uplift models.
  • Interpretable or accurate? Logistic models explain; boosted models predict better. Many teams deploy the boosted model and use the simpler one to explain.
  • Act on the riskiest? Customer-success teams often feel they must try with the riskiest accounts. The economics may say otherwise; the answer is a test.
  • Discount dependence. Repeated offers may teach buyers to churn to get coupons. Long-term holdouts are the only way to see it.

Self-test

Make the case for sending offers to the riskiest decile even though the test says it loses money.

Show answerHide answer

If those buyers are high-value strategic accounts, or losing them has costs beyond their spend, such as sellers who anchor a category, the value of a save may be much larger than ₹2,500. Then recompute break-even with that value.

Weak

"Label users who stopped using the app and train a classifier."

Strong

Pick an observation date; features from behaviour before it; label = no purchase in a window after it, sized to the buying cycle and checked against how many 'churned' buyers return. Rung 3 · What ifICMLE

Your churn model's AUC drops from 0.91 to 0.82 when you validate on a later month. What happened?

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • How would you find the leaking feature?
  • Is 0.82 bad?

Weak

"Overfitting; add regularisation."

Strong

Likely leakage, features using information from the label window, or drift. Check feature timestamps against the observation date, look for features whose importance collapses on the time split, and rebuild them strictly from earlier data. 0.82 on a time split is the honest number, and may be fine; lift and the economics decide.

Rung 4 · DesignLeadIC

Design a retention programme that uses churn and uplift models.

Follow-ups, weak and strong answersHide
  • Where does each model fit?
  • How do you measure the programme?

Weak

"Target the riskiest customers."

Strong

Risk model for forecasting, staffing and understanding reasons; value-weighted risk for account management. For offers: a randomised test across deciles, an uplift model trained on it, targeting where expected uplift × value exceeds cost. A standing random holdout to measure the programme every month; long-term holdouts for discount dependence; monthly checks of calibration and uplift.

Weak

Accepts the number.

Strong
